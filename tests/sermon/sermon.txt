Now, today we are going to be talking about strength for the waiting season. Strength for the waiting season. Because many of us are in a season where we have prayed, we have fasted, we have done everything we know how to do, and it looks like nothing is moving. You are waiting for a job. You are waiting for a child. You are waiting for healing. You are waiting for that door to open. And the waiting is hard. The waiting is the part nobody likes to talk about.

Let me tell you a story. In August of two thousand and ten, in the Atacama desert in Chile, a copper and gold mine collapsed. Thirty-three men were trapped about seven hundred metres underground. Seven hundred metres, church. That is deeper than many of our tallest buildings are tall. Up above, their families gathered at the mouth of the mine. They set up tents. They called the place Camp Hope. Day after day the drills went down, and day after day there was nothing. No sound. No sign. Many people said, it is over, those men are gone.

Seventeen days. For seventeen days nobody up top knew if anybody down below was alive. And on the seventeenth day, a drill came back up, and taped to the end of it was a small piece of paper. Written on it in red were the words, we are fine in the shelter, the thirty-three of us. All thirty-three were alive.

But listen to me. Finding them was not the same as bringing them out. They still had to wait. They waited sixty-nine days in total before the last man came up in a small capsule they called the Phoenix. Sixty-nine days. And those men later said that what kept them alive down there was not only the food that was sent down the pipe. It was hope. It was knowing that somebody up there had not forgotten them.

Church, somebody here feels like you are seven hundred metres down. You are in the dark. You have been waiting, and it looks like nobody remembers you. I came to tell you this morning, heaven has not forgotten you. There is a drill coming down to where you are.

Now let us look at a man in the Bible who knew all about waiting. His name was Joseph. You remember Joseph. His father loved him, gave him a coat of many colours, and his brothers hated him for it. God gave him a dream, and that dream got him thrown into a pit. His own brothers sold him to traders, and he ended up as a slave in Egypt.

And even there, Joseph served faithfully, and God was with him. But Potiphar's wife lied about him, and Joseph, an innocent man, was thrown into prison. Think about that. He did the right thing and still ended up in prison. Some of you know exactly how that feels. You did the right thing, and it still went wrong.

In that prison, Joseph met two men from Pharaoh's house, the butler and the baker. Both of them had dreams, and Joseph interpreted their dreams. And he said to the butler, when it is well with you, remember me. Mention me to Pharaoh, and bring me out of this house. And the Bible tells us, yet did not the chief butler remember Joseph, but forgat him.

He forgot him. Joseph had his breakthrough in his hand, and the man who could help him simply forgot. Let us go to Genesis chapter forty-one, verse one.

And it came to pass at the end of two full years, that Pharaoh dreamed: and, behold, he stood by the river.

Two full years. Not two days. Not two weeks. Two full years after the butler forgot him. Can you imagine Joseph sitting in that prison, every morning thinking, maybe today, and every evening, nothing. Two full years.

But notice something. The Bible does not say that God forgot. Men forgot, but God did not forget. And when God's time came, Pharaoh had a dream that nobody in Egypt could interpret, and suddenly the butler remembered. In one day, Joseph went from the prison to the palace. In one day.

Somebody needs to hear that. What men delayed, God can accelerate in one day.

And many years later, when his brothers stood in front of him, afraid that he would take revenge, Joseph said something powerful. He said, you meant it for evil against me, but God meant it for good, to save many people alive. You meant it for evil, but God meant it for good.

That is why Paul could write in the book of Romans chapter eight, verse twenty-eight, and we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose. All things. Not some things. The pit, the prison, the two full years, they all work together for good.

Now I know what some of you are thinking. Pastor, that is easy to preach, but I am tired. You remember Elijah. After that great victory on Mount Carmel, when fire fell from heaven, one message came from Jezebel, and Elijah ran into the wilderness, sat down under a juniper tree, and asked God to take his life. He said, it is enough, take away my life. That was a great prophet, church. Even great men get tired in the waiting.

And what did God do? He did not shout at Elijah. He sent an angel who touched him and said, arise and eat. God fed him and let him sleep. Sometimes the most spiritual thing you can do in the waiting season is to eat and rest. Remember what we read in Psalm twenty-three, he maketh me to lie down.

And even Jesus felt the weight of waiting. When Lazarus was sick, Jesus waited two more days before he went. By the time he arrived, Lazarus had been dead four days. Mary and Martha said, Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died. And standing at that grave, the Bible tells us, Jesus wept. He wept with them. He knew he was about to raise Lazarus, and he still wept with them. That tells me the God who makes you wait is not a God who does not feel your pain. He feels it with you.

So what do we do while we wait? Let me give you three things quickly, and then we will pray.

Number one. Keep serving where you are. Joseph served in Potiphar's house, and he served in the prison. He did not wait for the palace to start being faithful. Whatever your hands find to do right now, do it well.

Number two. Keep your hope alive. The miners in Chile held on to hope. The prophet Jeremiah, writing in the darkest time of his nation, wrote in the book of Lamentations, chapter three, verse twenty-two to twenty-three.

It is of the LORD's mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.

New every morning, church. Whatever happened yesterday, this morning there are new mercies with your name on them.

Number three. Wait on the Lord, not on men. The butler forgot, but the Lord remembered. Remember our first reading this morning. They that wait upon the Lord shall renew their strength. Not they that wait on their uncle. Not they that wait on the government. They that wait upon the Lord.

There is a verse that says the vision is yet for an appointed time, and though it tarry, wait for it, because it will surely come. It will not tarry. Your appointed time is coming.

So I want to leave you with this. Your waiting is not wasted. The two full years were not wasted. The sixty-nine days in the mine were not wasted. God is working in the waiting. The same God who brought Joseph out of the prison in one day, the same God who brought thirty-three men up out of the earth, is the God who is working on your case right now.
